Keeping Your Santa Fe Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
