How Los Ranchos de Albuquerque Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
