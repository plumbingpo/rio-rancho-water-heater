The majority of tankless breakdowns announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see again and again in Rio Rancho, NM.

Rio Rancho Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Rio Rancho, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. A code tells us where to look, not what to replace. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Repair

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Tankless heaters look simple from the outside, but inside is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric tankless units carry their own risk, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

Calling a pro often costs less than trial and error. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors with water moving, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
